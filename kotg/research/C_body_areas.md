## 0. The core framework (applies to every area)

- **Demand v Ability.** This is his top-level theory. Pain or injury means demand went past ability, and training raises ability. He always adds that there is "no guarantee." Quotes: "Human bodies adapt. Just slowly." / "More ability = fewer problems." / "YOU HAVE NO GUARANTEES." (atg-every-regression-counts-lower, 2026-06-20). "Bodies adapt. Just slowly. And not infinitely." (atg-pain-free-running-and-sprinting, 2025-10-19).
- **Gradient scales / "Every Regression Counts".** Every exercise gets levels from "grandma to NFL." Quote: "'Timeless abilities on a gradient scale.' That's the best I can summarize the ATG system thus far." (atg-jonathan-edwards-timeless-hamstring, 2026-11-19).
- **Never work through pain.** Quote: "Pain is a communication, and I want you to work in the zone that does NOT hurt." (atg-family-full-squat-example-with, 2026-09-25). Also "muscle burn - not knee pain" (atg-how-to-use-a-slant-to-decline, 2026-09-15).
- **Ground up.** Train feet, lower legs and sled first, then knees, hips, spine, and upper body last. Quote: "If two trees have the same branches, but one has stronger roots, which do you think will last longest?" (the-atg-longevity-system, 2024-11-27). The 2026 version adds **"rebuild from the ground"**, meaning resisting a load that sits on the ground, such as a sled or a weight too heavy to lift. That way the joint is challenged without going through a painful range (atg-every-regression-counts-shoulders, 2026-06-17; BoxRox summary of the "Ground Up" video, 2026-07-09, https://www.boxrox.com/?p=247400).
- **Balanced exercise.** Forward AND backward, fast AND slow, high AND low, strength AND flexibility ("stretch-strength") (athletic-longevity-series-1-what, 2025-03-22).
- **Enjoy it, in moderation, over years.** Quote: "when in doubt: do less and ENJOY it." (atg-biggest-low-back-misunderstanding, 2026-07-12).
- **Origin story he repeats:** a 1970s university study (he says "In 1978 a university found that more knees over toes = more pressure on knees") led to textbooks mandating "no knees over toes". He calls it "an understandable mistake," not malice (atg-family-full-squat-example-with, atg-mary-smith-breaks-another-world).

## 1. Knees

**Diagnosis.** The lost abilities are **downhill/downstairs** and **strength below the chair or below the game**. Avoiding knees-over-toes for decades left those ranges weak. Quote: "The main survival ability people lose in relation to age and knees over toes is DOWNHILL/DOWNSTAIRS." (atg-mary-smith-breaks-another-world, 2026-10-25). Quote: "A deep squat is the single best overall knee injury preventer I've seen over these 16 years" (atg-every-solution-counts-9-knee, 2026-06-21).

**Solution sequence.** He gives two versions.
- *Knee Ability Zero order* (atg-kot-calf-raise-boring-but-valuable, 2025-10-05): straight-leg calf raise, then tibialis raise, then knees-over-toes (bent-knee) calf raise, which holds the knee isometric, then "FINALLY start moving the knees and then hips." He frames it as "Ground-up Approach to Knee."
- *The "$9 knee" big three* (atg-every-solution-counts-9-knee, 2026-06-21): (1) **stepdown progression**, (2) **split squat mobility progression**, (3) **full squat strength progression** using heel elevation plus counterbalance. Quote: "the three most helpful knee over toes movements I have coached over my 16 years." Equipment: a $2 concrete block plus a $5 2"x6"x5' board.
- Backward walking or sled usually comes first. "backward progression is a regression to the problem of shin angle, as you work on your toes going behind your knee rather than your knee going over your toes" (atg-speed-truth-part-1, 2026-08-05).

**Key exercises and scaling levers.**
- **Backward sled or backward resisted treadmill.** This is what got him off painkillers. "the first thing that I could tell that allowed me to get off the painkillers was dragging a sled backwards" (Tim Ferriss #835 transcript, https://tim.blog/2025/11/13/ben-patrick-kneesovertoesguy-transcript/).
- **Stepdown / slant step-down.** Easier with the off-leg farther behind, assisted from the bottom foot, less reach, or standing lower. "Farther behind you is less knee pressure." (atg-how-to-use-a-slant-to-decline, 2026-09-15). The Poliquin-derived **Petersen step-up** works as a bridge from lower leg to upper leg (atg-shin-ability-exercise-routine, 2024-07-24).
- **Counterbalanced full squat.** Three dials: "How far down? How much weight? WHERE is the weight?" Quote: "the farther the weight is in front of you, the less knee pressure there is!" (atg-lifetime-squat-knee-health-gems, 2026-11-14). Progression: plate at arm's length, then dumbbell or kettlebell, then barbell reached out, then front, then back. Alternatives are the hex bar on a podium, the Zercher, and a $4 sandbag. Assistance with a band or cable counts as an "assisted hack squat."
- **"Hooper Therapy"**: 5x10 *bottom-half* reps with 30–60 s rest, for basketball players whose legs "almost never bend below halfway" (atg-hooper-therapy, 2026-04-04).
- **Banded or chained squats** are his preferred style for combining knee health with peak jump, since they get "appropriately heavier as it goes up" (atg-squat-depth-solution-for-athletes, 2026-07-26).
- He also lists hamstring curls as a knee remedy (see Sprinting).

## 2. Lower back

**Diagnosis.** Three themes:
- "The #1 way to injure your lower back is bending over, usually to pick something up." (atg-further-back-extension-accessibility, 2026-06-22).
- Lost mobility is the underlying cause. Quote: "THE PRIMARY CAUSE OF LOWER BACK PAIN is the LOST MOBILITY which puts the lower back in a stressful position in the first place!" There are three ways to pick something up without overstressing the back: "knee over toes / upper hamstring flexibility / knees widening" (atg-big-3-around-and-underlying-the, 2025-06-26).
- The biggest misunderstanding is **time**. Gravity never goes away, so the back needs lifelong basics. Quote: "TIME IS THE #1 MISUNDERSTANDING I STILL SEE IN PEOPLE WITH LOWER BACK PROBLEMS." / "You're never going to escape gravity as long as you live." (atg-biggest-low-back-misunderstanding, 2026-07-12; this is likely the "Missing Lower Back Understanding I'm Still Seeing" video, inferred). He also notes: "Our lower backs would have less pressure if we walked on all fours."
- He calls the back "connected." An ankle sprain once caused a back tweak during a squat. Quote: "'Your lower back health relies on the ability of your lower back and its supporting factors to handle your demands in life.'" He rejects both "your back only needs this one thing" and "your back problem is not a back problem" (atg-further-back-extension-accessibility).

**"Big 3 UNDERLYING" solution** (2025-06-26): the **ATG split squat** (ankle, deep knee and hip flexor length), the **seated dumbbell deadlift** (inner-thigh flexibility plus low-back strength), and the **standing dumbbell deadlift / full-stretch RDL** (upper hamstring). The "Big 3 AROUND" standards are: 10 slow back extensions with 25 lb (women) or 45 lb (men); 20 knees-to-triceps hanging raises; 45° side bends.

**Back extension: three abilities** (atg-back-extension-guide-with-or, 2025-10-18): **resist** rounding, **round**, and the rarely trained third, **come OUT of a rounded position**. Form: "First, flex the abs… Then round the body. Then slowly come OUT of a rounded position. Fully squeeze your butt muscles to finish the rep." He prefers kettlebell loading ("FARMER STRENGTH"). QL raises progress from low hand, to hand behind head, to holding weight. Options without a machine: elephant walk (flexibility), ham roller (extension), and seated QL. Quote: "a back extension machine is one of the very few pieces of machinery I wouldn't want my family to live without." He uses 10–20 reps holding 50–100 lb of sandbags. He says the floor or wall hold "has been used in physical therapy for over 60 years."

**Weekly minimum spine "set"** (atg-every-regression-counts-lower, 2026-06-20). This is a fatigue-based drop set on a horizontal back extension: (1) brace and hold with 100 lb, (2) drop one bag and do full flexion with 50 lb, (3) drop the bag, go single-leg and rotate, (4) turn to the side for QL.

**"Spine Day" in ATG Basics** (atg-lower-body-basics-on-a-budget, 2026-05-08): single-leg back extension (start and finish on the weaker side), full split squat, QLs. He used this for a "$100M+ contract" baseball player with one-sided lower-spine overdevelopment.

**Mapping to the "Lower Back Series 1: Top-Down / 2: Hips / 3: Direct" titles (inferred):**
- *Top-Down* fits "Timeless Ability ABOVE Your Lower Back" (2026-11-21): raise the arms overhead from a full squat, a lesson from Poliquin, who got it from Klokov. Quote: "When you raise your arms from a full squat, you have no choice but to use the muscles above your lower back, which help protect it." Levels: tummy time, heel-elevated squat with arms up, broomstick or PVC, loaded PVC (ankle weights or small plates), 15-lb bar for kids, then a loaded bar or snatch. "Top down" also appears in "back extension and ATG split squat – each progressed from the top down" (atg-longevity-exercise-basics, 2026-05-11).
- *Hips* most likely means hip flexor lengthening via the split squat, which "attach into the front of your lower spine."
- *Direct* most likely means back extension, QL, and seated DB deadlift. He says "the two most valuable skills I've found for direct low back strengthening are seated dumbbell deadlifts and knowing how to use the back extension machine" (atg-9-recent-coaching-lessons, 2025-12-11).

## 3. Hips

**Diagnosis.** "The lack of sprinting and too much chair-sitting not only means weakened knees and back, but also WEAK AND STIFF HIPS." (atg-longevity-exercise-basics). The hip flexors are "opposite of your lower back muscles and attach into your lower spine. Stiffness here also pulls you into a more stressful lower back position!" (atg-big-3). He calls the doctor's diagnosis of "front of the hip too tight, backside too weak" a match for the split squat plus back extension, his "Modern Pelvic Solution" (back-extension guide).

**Key exercises.**
- **ATG / full split squat** ("Knees Over Toes Lunge" written for F45's 1,000+ gyms, knees-over-toes-lunge-coaching-guide, 2025-10-16). Front-foot elevation sets the difficulty, the back heel stays straight or turned out for more hip flexor stretch, the back foot stays on its toes, the back knee doesn't touch, "real work… from about 90 degrees downward," and dumbbells are the loading of choice. The advanced step is a walking deep lunge. Quote: "With one exercise, we can build lost ability below the chair while restoring lost flexibility in the hips!" He has done it about once a week for 13 years, and his mother for 8.
- **Lateral and other planes:** Cossack squat, standing pancake, couch stretch, pigeon pose (optional, "folks who sit on the floor don't tend to need this one as much"), and the seated DB deadlift for the inner thigh (atg-mobility-made-simple, 2026-04-06; atg-lower-body-basics-on-a-budget).
- **Hip flexor strength:** L-sit (bent, then one leg straight, then both), hanging knee raise to triceps, then straight legs; and for speed, the machine "Case Curl" and the cable "Jamaican Hip Flexor" (see Sprinting).
- Tim Ferriss #835: "that split squat, she credits with fixing her hip problems" (his mother).

## 4. Shoulders

**Diagnosis.** "The #1 way to hurt your shoulder? Throwing." The goal is "MEASURABLE INCREASE OF ABILITY IN REVERSE OF DEMAND." Pressing is usually overdone compared with pulling. Quote: "when shoulders make lives worse, THEY USUALLY STARTED WITH A GOAL! Throw 100 mph! Bench 315! Intent is the DRIVER. ATG is the BALANCE." (atg-4-shoulder-health-tests-and-skills, 2025-04-06).

**4 shoulder "tests"** (2025-04-06):
1. **Powell Raise.** Poliquin named it after Mike Powell. Target: 10% of bodyweight for 8 strict reps, 4 s down.
2. **External rotation.** Same target: 10% BW x 8, 4 s down. Exercises 1 and 2 together approximate a cable "reverse of throwing" movement.
3. **Row matching bench.** Row your bench weight for the same reps and tempo, 3 s down and 1 s pause.
4. **Seated shoulder press through full natural range.** 25% BW per hand x 8 (or 25% of max bench), 3 s down, 1 s pause.

**"Reverse Out Shoulder Pain" / ground-up version** (atg-every-regression-counts-shoulders, 2026-06-17). This most likely matches the "3-Step Reverse Out Shoulder Pain Routine" video, inferred. Load progresses from arm only, to soup can, to light dumbbell and up. Full-range standard: "1-3 sets of 6-8 reps with 4 seconds down on each rep, 1-3 times per week, has been my go-to formula for over a decade." If range of motion hurts, put "way too heavy a weight on the ground" and try the same two motions, holding the top for up to 4 s. Quote: "If range of motion hurts, we have a strategically safe solution by resisting FROM THE GROUND." The same idea applies to the back (lift off the floor with hand assistance) and knees (sled "until the sled barely moves"), per the BoxRox write-up of "How to Rebuild Your Shoulders, Lower Back, AND Knees From The Ground Up" (2026-07-09).

**Rings routine** (atg-my-up-to-date-rings-routine, 2025-09-15): pull-ups, rows and face pulls, one set each to burnout at least once a week. The row is "my overall favorite upper body exercise," and the face pull is "the most underrated exercise I've seen" for throwers. Pull-up scale: feet assist up and down, then feet assist up only, then unassisted (atg-simplified-pull-up-scale). He also uses full-ROM push-ups and dumbbell overhead presses.

## 6. Feet, ankles, Achilles, shins

**Diagnosis.** "If you have pain in your foot, Achilles or shin: Your DEMAND in life exceeded your ABILITY to handle it." People "dramatically underestimate IMPROVING ABILITY FROM THE GROUND UP" (atg-simplified-foot-and-lower-leg, 2025-10-26). Athletes overdevelop everything above the lower legs, never do bent-knee calf work because of "no knees over toes," then add maximal running and jumping. "Doesn't that sound like a recipe to create lower leg pain?" (atg-lower-legs-simplified, 2025-06-21). Shoes: "The footwear industry profited by making shoes that are NOT SHAPED LIKE FEET." His footwear rules: barefoot is best, modern surfaces call for shoes, cushion depends on the activity (atg-footwear-simplified-to-1-2-3).

**Simplest version (2025):** "1) some form of forward and backward exercise, plus 2) some form of calf strengthening through your full range of motion." Forward loads the tibialis. Backward loads the soleus and the foot.

**Lower Legs Simplified (school version):** straight-leg and bent-leg calf raises, 5 reps each with a 5 s hold at the top, heels all the way down. Progression: two legs assisted, two unassisted, one assisted, one unassisted, one loaded.

**5-Step Shin Ability formula** (atg-shin-ability-exercise-routine, 2024-07-24). He says "This is what fixed my chronic shin splints":
1. Backward sled or backward running, 200 yd, "push through your big toes."
2. Soleus (seated or bent-knee) raise, 2x25.
3. Tibialis raise, 2x20 (tib bar or wall).
4. Standing calf raise, 2x15.
5. Petersen step-up, 2x20.

**Ankle Ability routine** (2024-06-27): floss band, backward toe-pushing, seated calf, tib raise, wall calf and tib rotations, reverse step-up paired with ham curls, ATG split squat paired with a toes-up staggered RDL.

**Other:** 4 slant positions (2025-08-09), including "Into the slant for foot-Achilles-calf" (slant calf raise). The KOT calf raise goes to burnout (about 25 reps). Pros should buy a seated calf machine. In Speed 4/5 he notes that Bolt used standing calf and seated tibialis machines, and Su Bingtian used standing and seated calf. Quote: "my main message is to find whatever lower leg methods you can genuinely enjoy and stick with." He credits Bob Gajda for the free-weight tibialis (Ferriss; atg-knee-ability-series-4). The "ATG Foot, Ankle, Achilles & Shin Guide" video wasn't directly accessible, but the posts above are almost certainly its substance.

## 7. Sprinting ("Speed Truth" 5-part series, Aug–Sep 2026; "Pain-Free Running & Sprinting Manual," Oct 2025)

**Diagnosis.** Adults stop sprinting, and "The further removed from sprinting, the riskier it gets." Electric treadmills are dangerous for sprinting. Quote: "Statistically: Most adults will spend most of their lives never sprinting!" (atg-speed-truth-part-2). Hamstring pulls are the top sprint injury. Hip flexors and lower legs take huge forces.

**Series structure:**
- **Part 1, start and shin angle** (2026-08-05). Su Bingtian has the fastest-ever 30 m (3.73 s) and 60 m (6.29 s) and squatted 2.6x bodyweight with knees over toes. Quote: "'knees over toes is a NATURAL ABILITY which you can train on a gradient scale.'" Tools: counterbalance squats, stepdowns, backward work.
- **Part 2, hamstrings** (2026-08-12). He does both machine curls and Nordics in a "Week A/B" rotation. He can curl 100% of BW for 4 reps. Do RDLs AND curls. "Exercise is not a political election." Answering "Just sprint": "I can't find a single elite sprinter who doesn't support sprinting with some form of hamstring strengthening." Noah Lyles does Nordics, and Su used the machine curl.
- **Part 3, hip flexors** (2026-08-16). The **Case Curl**: sit on a lying ham-curl machine with the pad on the knees and drive the knee up. The **Jamaican Hip Flexor** uses a cable standing on a step. Hanging knee raises go to 20, then straight legs. Test or train every 14 days with a 5–10 rep bracket. His numbers: ham curl 100% BW x 8, hip flexor 50% BW per leg x 6. Don't go to failure in the first session.
- **Part 4, lower legs** (2026-08-24). Standing calf, seated calf, seated tibialis. Quote: "When you put maximum force into the ground, your lower legs take a beating."
- **Part 5, reflective** (2026-09-03). "we all start somewhere."

**Pain-free return-to-running sequence (7 steps, 2025-10-19):** (1) Demand v Ability, (2) backward walking, then jogging, then running ("The effort to go backward drives the body's ability to protect itself when going forward!"), (3) sled, (4) "Zero" calf and tib positions, 20 reps building to 1 leg, (5) resisted (non-motorized) treadmill forward and backward, "my most common form of sprinting" for 3 years, (6) hamstrings: deadlift or RDL "with intent on hamstrings flexibility is the #1 helper I've seen", plus ham roller 2 legs, then 2-up-1-down, then 1 leg, (7) hip flexors via L-sit and full split squat. His mother sprints at 71. Hills: "Fast up, down slow" (atg-mobility-made-simple).

## 8. Jumping / dunking

**Diagnosis.** "I can't change your height. I can't change your genetics… But number 3 is KNEE PROBLEMS. And I CAN change your knees." (how-to-get-your-first-dunk, 2024-07-31). He also says "a max jump is NOT a full squat. Your ability below the jump is a missing zone of leg and knee SUPPORT. It does not replace the art and adaptation of the JUMP." (atg-volleyball-winning-with-full, 2026-09-07).

**5-part dunk plan (2024):**
1. Deep knee strengthening: backward walking, backward step-up, deep split squat, then deep squat "CONTROLLING the last quarter".
2. A foam, palm-able ball so you can swing your arms.
3. Adrenaline: group settings, competition, sleep, and saving caffeine for dunk day.
4. Lower legs: standing calf, seated calf, tibialis.
5. Posterior chain: Nordics, full-stretch RDL, ham curls, back extensions.

He calls this "all the main lower body exercises I'll do for the rest of my life."

**Other points:**
- **Poliquin's "#1 exercise for vertical jump"** is the wide-grip deadlift from a podium into a deep squat ("deep deadlift"), heels elevated, done with a hex bar for safety. His 12-week rotation cycles deep front squat, deep back squat and deep deadlift through 3x8-10, 4x6-8, 5x4-6 and 6x2-4. He reports "300-pound deep squats" (atg-1-exercise-for-vertical-jump, 2026-02-18).
- **Louie Simmons kneeling jumps** (2026-11-13; "Louie Simmons Kneeling Jump & Snatch Gradient Scale" video). Scale: assisted, unassisted, no arm swing, loaded (front), broomstick snatch, barbell snatch. Simmons quote: "This here will build some tremendous jumping ability." Ben's quote: "Joints adapt, but if you use too great or too sudden a force, they can get hurt."
- He has dunked for 13 years without a knee setback. Volleyball example: SMU's Natalia Newsome, whose coach Anthony Cockrill uses a 25-lb thick bar.
- Basketball: full squat is "by far the most important exercise for basketball players" because the game never goes below halfway (atg-9-recent-coaching-lessons).

**Not found:** the specific content of "The Jump Video I Wish I Had Growing Up" or of the Simon Bungate video. The posts above are probably close.

## 9. Olympic lifting / snatch

The material is limited, and he presents the lifts as an endpoint of gradient scales, not a core part of the system:
- The full split squat "was an abandoned method of catching the bar in OLYMPIC WEIGHTLIFTING" (athletic-longevity-series-1-what).
- Olympic lifting gives "a deep squat - but violently," which is why he values *controlled* deep work (knees-over-toes-lunge-coaching-guide).
- The overhead-from-full-squat progression, from tummy time to broomstick to bar, gives "a great foundation" to progress the snatch. The Klokov-to-Poliquin lineage is the source (atg-timeless-ability-above-your-lower, 2026-11-21).
- The kneeling jump scale ends with the broomstick snatch, then the barbell snatch.
- His new weekly split: "Monday Gymnastic / Bodyweight, Wednesday Olympic / Free Weight, Friday Bodybuilding / Machine."

## 10. Longevity / aging

**Diagnosis.** In places where the elderly don't break down easily, he sees two differences: "Still going up and down HILLS" and "GARDENING (with DEEP knee and back bending!)" (atg-longevity-exercise-basics, 2026-05-11). Vicious cycle: "With aging comes reduced ability to VIGOROUSLY use the body, due to fear… Yet the LACK of vigorous use results in even further loss." Quote: "Youth is flexibility. Adulthood is strength. Aging is loss of both!" (the-atg-longevity-system, 2024-11-27).

**Longevity system sequence (2024):** sled first, then ground-up lower legs, then stretch-strength (ATG split squat), then spine, then core "from the ground up" (feet as the resistance point), then posture-focused upper body, then grip to finish.

**Parents' two-step (2026-07-18):** (1) **"Joint-friendly cardio that STRENGTHENS"**: sled, air bike, rower or resisted treadmill, or free alternatives like walking uphill briskly and downhill slowly with small steps, or a stepdown board. (2) **"DEEPER ability"**: heel-elevated, counterbalanced squat. Quote: "How 'right' are you about your training methods if you can't get your parents to EXERCISE?"

**Mobility Made Simple (2026-04-06), the "5 fundamentals".** This likely matches the "8-Step Longevity Workout" (inferred). Forward and backward hill or treadmill 4x10 s, deep lunge progression, lateral deep lunge, deep slow back extension, optional hex squat, plus optional calf raise, pigeon pose, and an upper circuit of row, push-up and face pull. Quote: "This is the simplest training in my 18 years of coaching people."

**Timeless Basics (2026-11-21):** "For longevity, I think if you did just these 3 exercises well, you'd be in an elite category of knee and spine protection". The three are overhead-from-squat, the squat progression, and the back extension progression. "Radiating strength effect": strength built in a comfortable range carries over beyond it.

**Proof cases:**
- **Mary Smith** broke the age 85–89 200 m world record and "still doing knees over toes." For her he prescribes backward walking (hand-held with a forward-facing partner), backward sled, and the stepdown (atg-mary-smith-breaks-another-world, 2026-10-25).
- His parents (69 and 72), "almost a decade into working at ATG Basics."
- "Nana" attempting a 1,000 lb backward drag "unharmed."
- A 70-year-old woman playing pickup basketball (Oliver Xu's mother, 2026-11-11).
- Hackenschmidt jumped chairs until 85.

Ferriss: "my biggest mistake was treating it as short-term rehab." He suggests about two 45-minute sessions a week, 1–2 working sets.

## 11. Sitting / posture

**Diagnosis.** "In an overly sitting, underly sprinting society, the average person is weaker than natural when the backside muscles extend the body." Chairs cause lost strength "BELOW THE CHAIR," shortened hip flexors, and stiff hips. He says chair-sitters usually need heel elevation to squat deep.

**Solution.** The "1-2 punch" (inferred match to "1-2 Punch Directly in Balance to Modern Sitting") is almost certainly the **ATG split squat plus back extension**, each "progressed from the top down." This pairing appears repeatedly: "Modern Pelvic Solution" (back-extension guide), longevity basics, and Spine Day. Other pieces: floor sitting ("Floor sitting is like nature's mobility routine"), and rows "for the modern posture." His school policy is a strict **"Minimum 1:1 ratio of time NOT SITTING IN THE CLASSROOM"** (the-atg-school-one-page-policy-list, 2026-04-29; atg-phys-ed-series-1, 2025-02-22).

## 12. Youth athletes

**Diagnosis.** Early specialization and "mileage abuse": "I've never observed a basketball coach who didn't abuse mileage." Jordan, Kobe, LeBron and Curry all specialized late. Swimmer Cam McEvoy cut from 30–70k m a week to 2k and set a world record. Proposed formula: team court time 3 days a week, "with MORE FOCUS" (atg-a-mistake-made-by-nearly-all, 2026-07-02). High school sports are "one of the top sources of KNEE SURGERY."

**Solutions:**
- **Delay "working out"** in favor of play, playgrounds and gymnastics. Kids "load it INTENSELY, but through landing forces." First squat teaching uses a front-loaded sand kettlebell, with no rack (atg-at-what-age-should-you-learn, 2025-05-13; atg-lifetime-squat-knee-health-gems). Quote: "if you play basketball, NOT LEARNING IT HAS MORE RISK THAN LEARNING IT."
- **Youth Development System, 4 goals** (2024-11-12): single-leg squat x10, Nordic-type hamstring lowering x5, L-sit 20 s, pull-ups x10. Add-ons: sled, wall calf and tib, back extension and QL, rows, push-ups, overhead press, and a flexibility circuit.
- **In-season high school basketball** (2025-12-10): 3 optional workouts a week x 3 exercises x 1–3 sets. A: full squat, ham roller, pull-up. B: slant step-up, hanging knee raise, DB shoulder press. C: single-leg calf, back extension, inverted row. About 80% of students opt in. Coaching rules: rep brackets ("Get 10? Go up… Get 5 or less? Go down"), fewer reps with pauses, measurable goals, earning buy-in (he dunked in front of the team) (atg-9-recent-coaching-lessons). Don't wait for jumper's knee to teach the fix. Teach seated DB deadlift and back extension before deadlifts.
- **School gym:** "Rotate different sports each day. Finish each day with just 1-2 optional exercises." Swedish ladder is "my #1 recommendation for schools." Ham rollers stack well for groups. A fabric "Buddy Sled" lets partners train without weights.
- **"Human Basics > THE SPORT MASTERY > Further Customization"** (atg-how-basics-get-missed-in-sports, 2026-06-23). Example: Aroldis Chapman throws 100+ mph near 40 while full squatting.

## 13. Bodyweight vs free weights vs machines

His position is **"both/and," never "either/or,"** and he explicitly rejects "tear-down" social media.
- Quote: "The simple TRUTH is that both the bodyweight and machine versions can get the job done!" (Edwards post). This probably matches the video "TRUTH About Bodyweight, Free Weights, And Machines" (inferred).
- **Machines:** strengths are "precise measurement!" and easy variety. They're the best tool for elite strength in the ham curl, seated calf, back extension and Case Curl. Weakness: "Machines start out with some inherent load, whereas your leg alone starts out much easier. This makes the free weight version advantageous for rebuilding from very low levels! And long term, machines challenge elite strength." (ankle-ability-exercise-routine). Calibration differs between machines, so track progress on the same one. A back extension machine is "the most consistent bridge from 'UNABLE' to 'ABLE'."
- **Bodyweight:** free, scales from a 3-year-old to "1 in a million," and is good enough for "the CURRENT FASTEST MAN IN THE WORLD" (Lyles, Nordic). Rings and the Swedish ladder suit all ages.
- **Free weights:** prefer dumbbells for split squats ("they get safer as you go lower"), hex bar over barbell for failure safety, kettlebells for back extensions, $4 sandbags for longevity.
- **Weekly split (Nov 2026):** Monday Gymnastic/Bodyweight, Wednesday Olympic/Free Weight, Friday Bodybuilding/Machine.
- **Cardio machines:** a non-motorized hill or resisted treadmill is "more fundamental" than bikes or rowers, though those are "AWESOME" too. He notes that every rower and air bike he donated to the school broke at least once, while his treadmills needed zero maintenance (atg-mobility-made-simple). He also says "It's about convenience from home - not saying a hill treadmill is better than a real hill" (atg-the-most-common-hill-treadmill). (Commercial caveat: he sells the treadmill, back extension, slants, podium and shoes, and says so.)
