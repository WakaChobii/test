# Ben Patrick (KneesOverToesGuy / ATG): Exercise Library & Programs

Research file B. Compiled 2026-11-21.

## Sources and confidence levels

- **[P] Primary.** Ben's own Substack posts. The coordinator gave me plain-text copies of all 120 posts (Jun 2024 to Nov 2026), and I grepped them directly. Citations use the post slug, which maps to `https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/<slug>`. His X threads (via threadreaderapp) and the Tim Ferriss #835 transcript are also primary.
- **[S] Secondary.** Fan, review and press sites: a1athlete.com, Boostcamp, BarBend, Garage Strength, exercisewithstyle, shreddeddad, BoxLife.
- **[U] Unverified.** Claimed online but not confirmed in any primary text I could reach.
- **Limits.** YouTube, Reddit and Medium were blocked or returned 403, so video-only material could not be checked directly.

His main idea runs through everything: **"Demand vs. Ability."** Pain means demand exceeded ability. Training builds pain-free ability "from the ground up" on gradient scales, and he never works through pain. In his words: "It would be better to do more reps at the level that doesn't hurt, than to push into ANY pain" ([P] atg-slant-squat-breakdown).

## 1. Core principles that govern every program

## 2. Signature exercise library

### 2.1 Backward walking and backward sled drag (also resisted or "hill" treadmill)
- **What it is.** Walking or dragging a sled backward, pushing through the toes and balls of the feet. Variants are a powered-off or non-motorized resisted treadmill, a real hill, and the fabric "Buddy Sled."
- **Why.** In backward walking the toes land first and the knee travels over the toes without load pressing down. He credits it with getting him off painkillers: "the first thing … was dragging a sled backwards" (Tim Ferriss #835). He describes it as "an optional helper to improve ability DOWNHILL" ([P] atg-travelhotel). It also loads the soleus and feet, while forward work loads the front of the shin ([P] atg-simplified-foot-and-lower-leg). He credits Louie Simmons with sled dragging ([P] atg-knee-ability-series-2-of-10).
- **Cues.** Push through the big toes ([P] atg-shin-ability). On real hills, go "fast up, down slow" with small steps ([P] atg-mobility-made-simple).
- **Regressions.** Walking backward in a pool, then unloaded backward walking, then partner walking.
- **Progressions.** Add resistance from the ground (sled or treadmill), then backward jogging and running.
- **Typical doses.**

| Dose | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 5 min | Opener | thread 1687396575583010816 |
| 200 yd | Shin Ability | [P] |
| 200 yd or up to 5 min | Parents | [P] |
| 100-200 yd warm-up | General | Tim Ferriss #835 |
| 10 min | Adults, physical fitness program | [P] atg-physical-fitness-program |
| 3 rounds forward and backward "to burn" | Minimalistic and Thrive programs | [P] |
| 4 x 10 s each way | Mobility Made Simple | [P] |
| 50-100 yd each way | Youth Programs #1 and #2, Knee Ability Pro | [P] |

- **Standard.** About 50% of bodyweight for around 40 m ([S] a1athlete standards).

### 2.2 Forward sled push
- Paired with backward work in almost every program as "Forward & Backward." Forward loads the tibialis/shin and drives hip extension.
- In Knee Ability Pro: "Heavy forward and backward sled: 50 yards each way" ([P] atg-knee-ability-series-3-of-10).
- Secondary prescription: 5 min, hands high on the poles, small steps ([S] Yahoo/Men's Journal).

### 2.3 Tibialis raise (wall version and tib bar)
- **What it is.** Dorsiflexion against resistance.
  - **Wall version:** butt against the wall, legs straight, lift the toes.
  - **Tib bar version:** seated, credited to Bob Gajda; the machine version is credited to Bert Sorin of Sorinex.
  - **Kettlebell version.**
- **Why.** "YOUR TIBIALIS IS YOUR FIRST LINE OF DEFENSE AGAINST BOTH CHRONIC AND ACUTE LOWER BODY INJURIES." It decelerates the foot, and he credits it with his one-foot dunks ([P] chapter-1-of-the-knee-ability-zero).
- **Cues.** Knees locked. Hold 2 s at the top and 2 s at the bottom. Wear shoes for safe footing; higher heels make it harder.
- **Scaling.** Stand closer to the wall (easier) or farther away (harder). Then free weight, then machine.
- **Doses.**

| Dose | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 25 consecutive reps, 2 s pauses | Knee Ability Zero step 1 | [P] chapter-1 |
| 20 reps | Wall, general | [P] atg-knee-ability-series-4 |
| 2 x 20 | Overcoming shin pain | [P] atg-shin-ability |
| 1 x burnout | Long-term maintenance | [P] atg-shin-ability |
| 2 x up to 15 | Knee Ability Pro | [P] |

- **Standard.** Tib bar 20% of bodyweight for at least 15 strict reps ([P] my-23-physical-standards). An older version is 25% for 5x5 ([S] a1athlete).
- **Variant.** "Tib rotations" on the wall, 10 per direction ([P] ankle-ability).

### 2.4 Calf raises: straight-leg, bent-knee/soleus, KOT calf raise, slant calf raise, seated calf
- **Straight-leg (gastrocnemius) and bent-leg (soleus).** The bent-leg version "more fully trains the lower, deeper calf muscle (the soleus) which helps protect the Achilles" ([P] atg-lower-legs-simplified).
  - Protocol: 5 reps with a 5 s hold at the top, heels all the way down.
  - Scale: two legs assisted, two legs unassisted, one leg assisted, one leg unassisted, then one leg loaded.
- **KOT (knee-over-toe) calf raise.** Against a wall with the knee pushed forward. This is an isometric knee position: "knee not even in motion yet!"
  - Two legs to burnout (about 25 reps), then one leg.
  - Difficulty scales with how far the knee reaches over the toes ([P] atg-kot-calf-raise-boring-but-valuable).
- **Slant calf raise ("into the slant").** Levels: two legs, two up/one down, one leg, one leg loaded ([P] atg-4-foot-positions). He calls it "my favorite calf exercise" (thread 1631405798986481665).
- **Seated calf raise.** Soleus emphasis. Free weight for at least 20 reps, or machine up to 20 ([P] ankle-ability).
- **Doses.**

| Dose | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2 x up to 25 | Soleus | [P] shin-ability |
| 2 x up to 15 | Standing calf | [P] shin-ability |
| 2 x up to 10 single-leg + 2 x up to 15 seated | Knee Ability Pro | [P] |
| 10 per leg | Physical fitness program | [P] |

- **Standards.** Single-leg standing 25% of bodyweight for at least 10 reps with no bounce. Seated 50% of bodyweight for at least 15 ([P] my-23-physical-standards).
- **"Trifecta."** Standing calf, seated calf and tibialis together "simplified the whole subject of lower leg training" ([P] atg-shin-ability; atg-speed-truth-4-of-5).

### 2.6 Patrick step / Peterson (Petersen) step-up / Poliquin step-up / reverse slant step-up / stepdown
These are one family of "downhill ability" moves.
- **Petersen step-up.** Named for Carl Petersen, physio to the Canadian ski team. Heel up throughout, slow, at least one hand supported, 2 x 20. "A fantastic bridge from lower leg to upper leg" ([P] atg-shin-ability).
- **Poliquin step-up.** Front heel elevated on a wedge or slant, 4-6 in step. Appears as "Poliquin Step-up 2 x 20" in Youth Program #2 ([P] atg-program-1-and-2). Secondary prescription: 3-6 x 15-25 per side ([S] Yahoo).
- **Patrick step.** Flat-ground knee-over-toe step-down with a light heel tap.
  - Standard 25 consecutive reps to full ankle bend; then elevated and loaded ([S] a1athlete).
  - The name "Patrick step" does **not** appear in his 2024-26 Substack. He now calls it "stepdown" or "reverse step-up."
- **Reverse / slant step-up and stepdown (current version).** The intent: "improve my ability to handle my body DOWNHILL without knee pain."
  - Three regressions: slope, size of step, and assistance.
  - Goal: "25 reps, heel in front of toes, on a slant." His own dose: "2 x 25 is like a push-up for epic knee ability" ([P] atg-simplified-step-ups-for-knee).
  - Lowest regression: off foot behind and pushing off the toes, moving gradually to in front. About 20 reps, slow down and fast up, 1-3x a week ([P] atg-every-regression-counts).
  - Budget version: a 2x4 under the heel ([P] atg-lower-body-basics-on-a-budget).
- **Standards.** Reverse step-up on a slant, at least 20 reps with a 6" step ([P] my-23-physical-standards). Poliquin step-up 66% of bodyweight for 20 ([S] a1athlete, older).
- **Doses.**

| Dose | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 3 x 20 | Knee Ability Pro | [P] |
| 2 x 25 | Minimalistic | [P] |
| 25-50 "to pump" | Athletic Flow | [P] |
| 2 x 20 | Ankle Ability | [P] |

- **Starting step height.** About 2-3 in if 6 in hurts, building to 6-8 in ([S] BoxLife, from the Ferriss episode).

### 2.7 ATG split squat (full / deep split squat, "Knees Over Toes Lunge")
- **What it is.** A front-foot-elevated deep split squat. The goal is the back of the front thigh covering the calf, the rear knee off the floor, and an upright posture ([P] atg-physical-fitness-program).
- **Why.** "The single best exercise investment I ever put in my body." He credits Poliquin for it. It trains ankle mobility and deep knee strength, stretches the rear-leg hip flexor (which matters for the lower back), and balances left and right ([P] atg-mobility-series-1-of-10; atg-big-3).
- **Cues** ([P] knees-over-toes-lunge-coaching-guide):
  - Hip-width stance.
  - Rear heel straight or out (out gives more hip-flexor stretch).
  - Stay on the rear toes throughout.
  - Back knee does not touch the floor; if it does, raise the front foot.
  - Natural torso; don't strain forward or back.
  - No lockout needed; the work is from 90 degrees down.
  - Don't overcorrect a lifting front heel.
- **Regressions.** Higher front foot (chair, stairwell, bumper plates, cinder block). Rail, broomstick or PVC for assistance.
- **Progressions.**
  - Lower front foot, then flat ground, then dumbbells ("they get safer as you go lower").
  - Then a walking full-ROM lunge (advanced).
  - [S] a1athlete adds back-foot elevation and barbell on the back or front rack.
- **Doses.**

| Dose | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 3 x 5 with a 5 s stretch | ATG Zero | [P] how-to-keep-or-rebuild-your-squat |
| 3 x 8 per side | Parents | [P] |
| 10 per side, up to 3 sets | Physical fitness program | [P] |
| 4 x up to 12 | Full-body twice weekly | [P] |
| 8 x 8 | Knee Ability Pro Thursday | [P] |
| 4 x 5, 3 s pause, before squats | Squat School | thread 1691554415470374963 |
| 30 s hold, stairwell | Opener | thread 1687396575583010816 |
| 5 x 5 | Boostcamp Zero | [S] |

- **Standard.** 25% of bodyweight per hand for at least 8 reps ([P] my-23-physical-standards).

### 2.8 KOT squat / heel-elevated (slant) squat / "low & slow" squat / counterbalance squat
- **Counterbalance.** Holding weight in front reduces knee pressure. Progression goes heavier and closer to the body: plate held far out, then plate near the knees, then DB/KB, then barbell front or back, then behind-the-back ("hack," after Hackenschmidt) ([P] atg-counterbalance-a-squat-skill; Ferriss #835).
- **Heel elevation.** "Useful but not vital." Options are a slant, a 2x4, or a rolled towel. He uses the slant "to emphasize the quads and knees, not just as a Band-Aid for ankle mobility" ([P] atg-slant-squat-breakdown).
- **Slant squat progression** ([P] atg-slant-squat-breakdown):
  1. 20 pain-free reps on two legs.
  2. Single-leg with two hands assisting.
  3. One hand assisting.
  4. Unassisted, 10 per side.
  5. Load it.
- **Assisted versions.** Between two chairs, or with a band or cable ([P] thread 1631405798986481665; atg-lifetime-squat).
- **Low and slow squat.** Knee Ability Pro Monday is 10 x 1, at least 10 s down each, with "PARTICULAR intention to slow down the last quarter." Squat School uses 4 x 2 with 10 s down. His standard is a full-control deep barbell squat, 100% of bodyweight for 6.
- **Doses.**

| Dose | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 3 x 10 | KOT squat, Knee Ability Pro | [P] |
| 5 x 5, 5 s down | Heels-elevated, Minimalistic | [P] |
| 3 x 5, 5 s down | ATG Zero | [P] |
| 3 x 10 | Low & slow, Youth #2 | [P] |
| 20 reps | Physical fitness program | [P] |
| 3 x 5 | Thrive | [P] |

- **Advanced.** Hex-bar "deep deadlift" on a podium (Poliquin's "#1 exercise for vertical jump"). He rotates front squat, back squat and deep deadlift through a 12-week cycle: 3x8-10, then 4x6-8, then 5x4-6, then 6x2-4 ([P] atg-1-exercise-for-vertical-jump).

### 2.11 Nordic hamstring curl and the ham curl system
- **Why.** "Makes a knee harder to rip apart." Hamstrings take the most force in sprinting ([P] atg-8-exercise-skills; atg-speed-truth-part-2).
- **Progression** ([P] updated-atg-ham-curl-nordic-system; atg-every-regression-counts-1f5):
  1. Ham roller (or cardboard or sliders) on 2 legs.
  2. 2 legs up, 1 leg down.
  3. 1 leg.
  4. Nordic negative: control down, push back up.
  - Reach distance sets difficulty: closer is easier.
  - Adjustable-angle Nordic benches and band assistance.
  - A strict bodyweight curl means no hand or hip assist.
- **Week A/B.** He alternates the machine and the Nordic. He also cites Jonathan Edwards' weekly Nordic on a Swedish ladder.
- **Doses.**

| Dose | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 3 x 5 eccentric | Knee Ability Pro | [P] |
| 3 x up to 5 Nordics, or 8 machine curls | Full-body twice weekly | [P] |
| 4 x 5, or 10-15 curls, after each squat set | Squat School thread | [P] |
| 5 reps | Physical fitness program | [P] |
| 2 x 8 | Minimalistic | [P] |
| 1 x 5 | Youth #1 | [P] |
| 2 x 10 roller | Thrive | [P] |

- **Standards.** Nordic, 5 full reps. Machine curl, 85% of bodyweight for at least 8 ([P] my-23-physical-standards).

### 2.12 Elephant walk
- **What it is.** A standing forward fold with hands supported. Alternate straightening one knee while the other bends.
- **Cues.** "Bend both knees. Straighten one … Bend both again. Straighten the other." It progresses "from higher to lower" hand position ([P] atg-back-extension-guide-with-or).
- **Doses.** Up to 20 per side ([P] childlike-mobility). 20 per side, 1-2 rounds ([P] full-body-atg-twice-per-week).
- **Standard.** "Elephant Walk Palms to Floor" ([P] my-23-physical-standards).
- Secondary dose: 1 x 36 in Boostcamp's week 1 ([S]). Part of his lifelong 4-part flexibility routine ([P] atg-8-exercise-skills).

### 2.13 Jefferson curl — [S] only
- Listed in older ATG standards as 25% of bodyweight for 10, wrists below toes ([S] a1athlete). a1athlete cues: chin tuck, segmental curl, 0-5 kg to start.
- **No mentions in 120 primary Substack posts.** His current spinal flexion work is done on the back extension instead (flexion with 50 lb, see 2.16).
- Treat it as legacy or community-attributed.

- **Cues** ([S] a1athlete/BoxLife): wide legs, abs to the bench rather than chest, shoulder blades retracted.
- **Doses.**

### 2.15 Couch stretch
- "My #1 most-used stretch over the last 10 years." He does it right after each of his 3 weekly workouts, and it is "the only stretch I do up to 3 times each week." Up to 45 s per side.
- **Cues.** Use a thick cushion or the KneeMat; a couch or bench is easier than a wall. Torso more upright and press against the wall to progress. Squeeze the glutes.
- **Pulse version.** 2 x 20 in Knee Ability Pro.
- **Doses.** 1 x 60 s in the Youth programs; 30 s in the opener thread.
- He pairs it with the ATG split squat for "flexibility OPPOSING YOUR LOWER BACK" ([P] childlike-mobility; 4-way-flexibility-routine).

### 2.16 Back extension (45-degree) with intentional flexion; reverse hyper
- **Order of a rep** ([P] atg-back-extension-guide-with-or):
  1. "First, flex the abs."
  2. "Then round the body."
  3. "Then slowly come OUT of a rounded position."
  4. Squeeze the glutes fully to finish.
  - Kettlebell is his favorite load.
- **Regressions** ([P] atg-every-regression-counts-lower; atg-further-back-extension-accessibility):
  - Horizontal bodyweight hold, from PT practice going back 60+ years, using a wall and padding. He suggests a 9-pack of toilet paper with the centre roll removed.
  - Handrail assistance.
  - Ham roller with cardboard (2 legs, 2-up/1-down, 1 leg).
  - Seated back extension (credited to Fernando Lopez).
- **His weekly "minimum set," 4 parts in a row:** bracing with 100 lb, then flexion with 50 lb, then single-leg rotation, then side-on QL ([P] atg-every-regression-counts-lower).
- **Doses.**

| Dose | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2 x 15 | Minimalistic | [P] |
| 2 x up to 20 | Full-body twice weekly | [P] |
| 20 | Home thread | [P] |
| 1-3 x 15 | Hoop Dreams | [P] |
| 2 x burnout, deep and slow | Mobility Made Simple | [P] |
| 10-20 with 50-100 lb sandbags | Ben himself | [P] |

- **Standards.**
  - 10 SLOW reps with 25 lb (women) or 45 lb (men) ([P] atg-big-3).
  - 25% of bodyweight for 20 ([P] my-23-physical-standards).
  - Single-leg with 100% of bodyweight ([S] older a1athlete).
- **Reverse hyper.** Not found in primary texts **[U]**. I could not verify a "reverse lower back extension" as a named ATG exercise.

### 2.17 QL extension / side bend (quadratus lumborum)
- **Variants.** Side-on on a back extension, 45-degree preferred ("less angle = easier"). Seated QL with a wall behind; standing for beginners.
- **Progression.** Lower hands, then hands behind the head, then holding weight ([P] atg-back-extension-guide; atg-big-3).
- **Doses.**

| Dose | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Seated, up to 10 per side | Childlike mobility | [P] |
| 2 x 10 | Knee Ability Pro | [P] |
| 1 x up to 20 | Full-body twice weekly | [P] |
| 1 x burnout | Minimalistic | [P] |

- **Standard.** 20 reps ([P] my-23-physical-standards). Expect days of soreness when new.

### 2.18 L-sit, Garhammer raise, hanging knee raise (hip flexors and lower abs)
- **L-sit.** Parallettes or cinder blocks. Progression: legs bent, then one leg straight, then both straight ([P] atg-pain-free-running-and-sprinting). Doses: 1 x 20 s in the Youth programs, 2 x burnout in Knee Ability Pro. Standard of 15 s on flat ground ([S]).
- **Hanging knee raise** ("my #1 ab exercise," [P] atg-my-1-ab-exercise).
  - Cues: balls of the feet on the floor to start, pull the elbows down, drive the knees to the triceps.
  - Goal 20 reps, then straighten the legs at the top.
  - Standard: "20 knees to triceps" ([P] atg-big-3).
- **Garhammer raise.** Standard of at least 20 as an alternative to the cable reverse squat ([P] my-23-physical-standards).

### 2.19 Hip flexor lift / psoas march / cable reverse squat / "Case Curl" / "Jamaican hip flexor"
- **Cable reverse squat.** Supine, ankles strapped to a low cable, knees to chest. Standard 50% of bodyweight for at least 20 ([P] my-23-physical-standards). Dose 1 x up to 20.
- **"Case Curl."** Seated in a lying ham-curl machine with the roller on the knees, lie back, drive the knee up. He tested 50% of bodyweight per leg for 6. He trains it every 14 days in a 5-10 rep bracket: hit 10 and go up, can't get 5 and go down ([P] atg-speed-truth-part-3-of-5).
- **"Jamaican hip flexor."** Cable on the foot, standing on a step ([P] same post).
- **Hip flexor lift.** Weighted foot on a ratchet/MonkeyFeet attachment, 3-4 x 20 per side ([S] Yahoo). The MonkeyFeet knee raise standard is 10% of bodyweight for 20 ([S]).
- **"Psoas march."** Not found as an ATG name **[U]**. "Banded hip flexion" is Garage Strength's own variant ([S]).

### 2.20 Side-lying hip abduction / ATG hip work
- **No primary mention of side-lying abduction [U].**
- His hip work is mainly:
  - **External hip:** the 90/90 or "piriformis push-up" (up to 20 per side), and the wall external hip stretch, where scooting farther away is easier.
  - **Internal hip:** rotation work up to 45 s or 20 reps.
  - **Groin:** standing pancake pulse (up to 20; standard is head to floor).
  - **Lateral and front:** Cossack squat (2 x 8 or 2 x 10), side split squat with plate counterbalance, and 4-way leg swings (1 x 10 each direction).
  - Sources: [P] childlike-mobility; atg-minimalistic; atg-hoop-dreams; atg-mobility-made-simple.

### 2.21 Pancake / standing pancake pulse
- "My favorite [groin exercise] … the wider my feet go, the STRONGER my groin muscles have to be." Up to 20 reps. A cinder block reduces range ([P] childlike-mobility; atg-lower-body-basics-on-a-budget).
- 2 x 20 in Knee Ability Pro. Standard is head to floor.

### 2.22 Slant board
- It is the one item he packs for travel: "the most bang-for-your-buck lower body tool." DIY dimensions are about 20" x 13" x 6", at 25 degrees, and at least hip width ([P] atg-travelhotel; atg-slant-squat-breakdown).
- **Four foot positions** ([P] atg-4-foot-positions):
  1. Full slant for step-ups.
  2. Partial slant for squats.
  3. No slant for split squats.
  4. Facing into the slant for calf and Achilles.
- **Slant toe touch** (backside flexibility): 1-2 x 20.

### 2.23 Tib bar
- A Bob Gajda (DARD) revival. Ben sells the "lowest price American-made tib bar" ([P] atg-knee-ability-series-4). Standard 20% of bodyweight for at least 15. See 2.3.

### 2.24 ATG shoulder work ("reverse of throwing")
- **Main principle.** "Getting measurably stronger, over time, in reverse of throwing."
- **Dose formula.** 1-3 x 6-8 with 4 s down, 1-3x a week: "my go-to formula for over a decade."
- **Regression ladder.** Just the arm, then a soup can, then a light DB. If range hurts, "resist FROM THE GROUND" (sled, or lifting from the floor) with up to a 4 s hold at the top ([P] atg-every-regression-counts-shoulders).
- **4 shoulder "tests"** ([P] atg-4-shoulder-health-tests-and-skills):
  1. Powell raise (named by Poliquin after Mike Powell): 10% of bodyweight for 8 with 4 s eccentrics.
  2. External rotation: 10% of bodyweight for 8 with 4 s eccentrics.
  3. Row matching bench: same load, reps and tempo (3 s down, 1 s pause).
  4. Seated full-ROM shoulder press: 25% of bodyweight per hand for 8, 3 s down and 1 s pause (or 25% of max bench).
- **Other upper-body staples.**
  - Pullover: 25% of bodyweight for 12, wall version first.
  - Face pull: 15-20.
  - Eccentric trap-3 raise: 10% per hand, 5 s down.
  - Full-ROM push-up and incline press.
- **Hangs.** The pull-up scale is hang, then hold the top, then lower slowly, then full reps. "Hanging alone is a fantastic exercise for the grip and shoulders!" ([P] how-to-start-training-your-parents). Simplified school version: feet assist up and down, then feet assist only up, then no assist ([P] atg-simplified-pull-up-scale).
- **"3-step reverse-out shoulder pain."** I could not verify this exact title **[U]**. Its content most likely matches the regression ladder above.

### 2.25 Jumps, sprints and the snatch progression
- **Sprint / run return** ([P] atg-pain-free-running-and-sprinting):
  - Progression: backward walk, backward jog, backward run.
  - Sled.
  - "Zero positioning" calf and tib work for 20.
  - Resisted treadmill sprints forward and back (his main sprinting for 3 years).
  - Full-stretch RDL and ham roller for hamstrings.
  - L-sit and full split squat for hip flexors.
  - 4-way running: 30 yd each of backward, left, right and forward, 2 rounds, walking back between reps ([P] atg-athletic-routine).
- **Jumps.**
  - 4-way jumping: 10 backward, 10 side to side, 10 forward, at least 30 s rest ([P] atg-athletic-routine).
  - Weekly "dunk day": about 20 near-max dunks with full recovery ([P] full-body-atg-twice-per-week).
- **Dunk system** ([P] how-to-get-your-first-dunk):
  1. Deep knee strengthening: backward walk, then backward step-up, then deep split squat, then controlled deep squat.
  2. Foam or palmable ball, so you can swing your arms.
  3. Adrenaline: group setting, competition, caffeine timing.
  4. Lower legs: standing calf, seated calf, tib.
  5. Posterior: Nordic, full-stretch RDL, ham curl, back extension.
- **Snatch / kneeling jumps** ([P] atg-louie-simmons-kneeling-jumps, Nov 2026, credited to Louie Simmons):
  - Kneeling jump from full knee bend, assisted.
  - Then unassisted.
  - Then without arm swing.
  - Then front-loaded.
  - Then a broomstick snatch.
  - Then a barbell snatch.
  - The overhead broomstick or loaded-PVC lift from a full squat is described as a "foundation for the snatch" ([P] atg-timeless-ability-above-your-lower).

### 2.26 Swedish ladder (stall bars)
- **Confirmed [P].** "The Swedish ladder is my #1 recommendation for schools." It was invented by Pehr Henrik Ling. He uses it for Nordics (heels hooked), hanging knee raises (his parents) and climbing (his kids). He doesn't sell one ([P] atg-jonathan-edwards-timeless-hamstring, 2026-11-19).
- Stall bars are also mentioned for lower-ab and hip-flexor progressions ([P] atg-physical-fitness-program). He was developing a squat rack that doubles as a pull-up bar and stall bars ([P] family-atg-rapid-study-series-press).

### 4.6 How he starts beginners ("How I start people")
I could not verify a post or video with this exact title **[U]**. His consistent primary protocols are below.
- **Parents/grandparents** ([P] how-to-start-training-your-parents). "Simpler is better":
  1. 200 yd or up to 5 min backward, partner style.
  2. 3 x 8 ATG split squats per side on a stairwell with the rail; most sets with the front foot elevated even when flat ground is possible.
  3. 2 sets of chin-up progression (hang, hold, lower, full) to fatigue.
- **Knee pain** ([P] Ferriss #835; atg-kot-calf-raise; atg-knee-ability-series-2).
  - Backward walking unloaded first.
  - Then lower legs: straight calf, tib, KOT calf.
  - Then stepdown at the lowest pain-free level.
  - Then the stairwell split squat.
  - Then a heel-elevated counterbalanced squat.
  - Start with 1-2 sets.
- **Youth.** Slant squat first, then the full split squat, then the reverse step-up before graduation. In rehab the order reverses: step-up, then split squat, then squat ([P] atg-slant-squat-breakdown).
- **Progression rule, in short.** Train at your pain-free level, often to burnout. When that is easy, move one notch along the scale (less assistance, lower, steeper, one leg, more load, load closer). If it hurts, go back a notch. Log progress, since the "standards" are measurable.
