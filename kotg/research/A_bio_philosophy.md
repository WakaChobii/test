## 1. Biography

### Childhood: the obsessive, stiff "Old Man"
- Grew up obsessed with basketball. elitefts recounts that at 4 he gave up toys to save for a $1,000 Michael Jordan rookie card (reached by 7), and trained with 5 a.m. dribbling jogs, ankle weights, plyometrics and YMCA games vs. adults, sometimes three times a day. [EFTS-BIO]
- Knee instability started around age 9 ("my knee would kind of pop out of place and sometimes it wouldn't go back in"), chronic pain by 12, a first serious injury around 14: "9, 12, 14, those are dates that come to mind." [MW]
- To Ferriss: "I had a high school basketball coach who started calling me Old Man. I was so stiff it would take me so long to warm up... I was just doing crazy workouts from the time I was maybe nine years old, so by 12, chronic knee pain... during all that puberty time, things weren't forming right, because I was so stiff I wasn't getting into my legs the way I should." [FERRISS]
- He went through high school "unable to grab the rim." [FERRISS] He described himself as slow and stiff: "The blessing was being a shit athlete. And being stubborn and thinking I'd somehow be able to dunk a basketball." [GS]

### Surgeries, painkillers, depression (around 18)
- "By 18, I then did have surgery, partial kneecap replacement. Part of my kneecap was just floating there. Quad tendon reattached and then had a meniscus transplant... I was immobilized and really couldn't even run for a year and a half... By then, my right knee was hurting worse than my left knee ever had." [FERRISS] (Rogan transcript confirms the meniscus transplant at 18 and quad tendon repair. [ROGAN])
- elitefts says the MRI showed a fractured kneecap he had probably been playing on since 14. It says the meniscus transplant was a "full" one and that he dropped out of school and became depressed. [EFTS-BIO] (Secondary source. Some other secondary bios say "three knee surgeries by 18"; Ben's own count is the three procedures above.)
- He stayed on painkillers in secret: "I had stayed on painkillers and my parents didn't know. My girlfriend didn't know, who's my wife now." [FERRISS] To Rogan: "I was hooked on painkillers, icing my knees four times a day just to go out every few months and try to play." [ROGAN]
- He was painting walls for his dad after high school with no scholarship: "people think you failed at life." [ROGAN]

### The Poliquin discovery and the backward sled
- "First clue was from a guy named Charles Poliquin... I was painting walls, researching relentlessly and I saw a clue from him about the farther and stronger your knee can go over your toes, the less chance of knee injury you have." [ROGAN]
- He couldn't find video of Poliquin, but "he had a disciple named Keegan Smith, an Australian strength coach... there I could see video examples of him doing it and they were intentionally training knees over toes." [ROGAN] (Keegan Smith later co-hosted the ATG Podcast and created ATG For Coaches, per podcast bios.)
- Trial and error: "I just started trying everything... what got me the least pain was actually the lowest level of quote unquote knees over toes training, which is basically dragging a sled backwards." He improvised by pushing his car backwards in neutral. [ROGAN]
- To Ferriss he named the specific article: Poliquin used backward sled work to rehab an Olympic athlete who then medalled. "That sold me on it." [FERRISS]
- He came off painkillers within about a week: "after the first week of doing this, I then intentionally got off. That didn't mean all my pain was gone... there's something different here of progressing the knee over the toes rather than avoiding the knee over the toes." [FERRISS]
- **The nature of the "mentorship":** Ben calls Poliquin "my mentor" [SUB, Louie Simmons post], but by his own account he learned mostly from Poliquin's articles. He saw him in person once: "only one time when I finally had the money and freedom to go see, he came to America, did a seminar." Poliquin said there "his only regret was not getting into flexibility sooner." [FERRISS] So "mentor" means intellectual mentor, not a long coaching relationship. Ben's praise: "I think Poliquin is the greatest genius in training ever," and he jokes he's been called "Poliquin for Dummies." [GS]
- On lineage, Ben credits backward walking to Chinese practice for the elderly, and sled dragging to Finnish lifters who dragged trees in forestry, popularized by Louie Simmons at Westside. Poliquin "went to the source." [ROGAN][FERRISS]
- **Keith Weber / "Weber method": NOT VERIFIED.** I found no source tying Ben Patrick to a Keith Weber or a Weber method across several searches and in the Ferriss, Rogan and Modern Wisdom transcripts. Treat this as unconfirmed or possibly a mix-up. The named influences I could confirm are Poliquin, Keegan Smith, Louie Simmons, Bob Gajda (tibialis/lower-leg training; "Mr. Universe right before bodybuilding really blew up" [FERRISS]), Dmitry Klokov (via Poliquin) [SUB], and Jerzy Gregorek (mentioned by Ferriss, not Ben).

### Comeback, JUCO, the D1 scholarship he walked away from
- "Manage to market myself and get a college basketball scholarship, played two years at a junior college... kept healing my knees while playing... got a full ride D1 scholarship at age 23." [ROGAN] elitefts adds he was a two-year starting point guard and captain and missed no games due to his knees. [EFTS-BIO]

Then the NCAA denied his eligibility ("your clock is up"). Rather than appeal, "I actually just withdrew from the thing and became a trainer." [ROGAN] (elitefts tells this a little differently, saying he turned down D1/D2 offers because strength coaches wouldn't allow his regimen. [EFTS-BIO] The Rogan first-person account is more specific.)
- He was already coaching hometown kids in summers: "I knew deep down, screw my career, I'm helping kids already." [EFTS-BIO]
- Dunking: "I'm 34, I've been dunking for over a decade without having any problem." [FERRISS] Ferriss's intro says he went from a sub-20" vertical to a "documented 42″ leap". That's a host claim, not independently verified. [FERRISS]

## 2. Core principles (verified wording)

### 2.1 The two founding principles (2022 formulation)
From Modern Wisdom, his cleanest statement:
> "principle number one: get stronger in reverse to the pain, because that's usually the simplest and the least painful thing you can do... principle number two: restore and train full range of motion, but at the level that doesn't hurt you. If you have only those two principles and if you then worked at it you could probably create a cool system for just about any area of the body." [MW]

This is the source of the "reverse out of pain/weakness" idea. Backward walking and sled work is the reverse of forward-walking knee pain. Reverse hypers and back extensions apply it to the back. Training decelerators and external rotators applies it to the shoulders. [MW][ROGAN]

### 2.2 The three training principles (2025 formulation)
To Ferriss he gave an updated three-part version:
> "it's just three total principles as far as I can see, which is the forward and backward resisted movement; and then the training from the ground up, just reminding myself even if it's one set, 'Okay, I'm going to hit the lower legs before I go to the upper legs'; and then the third one being the strength through my mobility, and then I just flow that to the upper body and I'm done." [FERRISS]
> "my whole philosophy is just to have balanced ability in the body, forward, backward, high positions, low positions, lower legs, upper legs." [FERRISS]

### 2.3 Knees over toes is an ability on a gradient, not a danger
- The old rule came from a correct observation (more knee pressure) and a wrong conclusion: "there was a gross misunderstanding of knee over toes equals more pressure on your knee, therefore avoid the area completely... human bodies... actually need motion and pressure... Motion and compression is how you get synovial fluid to carry nutrients to the joint. So we would think avoid bending a joint to make it last longer. It's the opposite. Now, just forcing into pain, that wouldn't make sense either." [ROGAN]
- 2026 framing: "Now that the mistake of 'no knees over toes' has been debunked, replaced with the understanding that it is a gradient scale of ABILITY, I see a bright future for full squat solutions for seniors!" [SUB, Lifetime Squat, 2026-11-14] https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/atg-lifetime-squat-knee-health-gems
- He blames a "1970s university misinterpretation (and subsequent mandate) of 'no knees over toes,' which prevented deep strengthening." [SUB, Hooper Therapy] https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/atg-hooper-therapy (This is his historical claim. Ferriss also refers to the 1970s-era convention.)

### 2.4 Never train through pain; regress until it's pain-free ("gradient scales")
- "I'm trying to help people get better at knees over toes, not work through pain in the process, gradually coax that ability or if they've already got it, we can fortify it super easily." [FERRISS]
- "It occurred because I figured out gradient scales and never had to work through pain, so my knees adapted without being beat up in the process." [SUB, Louie Simmons' Kneeling Jumps, 2026-11-13] https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/atg-louie-simmons-kneeling-jumps
- "(I worked through muscle burn - not knee pain - to dramatically reduce my knee pain!)" [SUB, Slant, 2026-09-15] https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/atg-how-to-use-a-slant-to-decline
- ATG program copy: "We never train through pain, and we always regress any exercise to a comfortable, pain-free level." and "every ingredient has smooth step-by-step levels, so we can meet you where you're at right now." [ATG]
- His job, as he sees it: "I'm just trying to play my part to be as obsessive as I can to master these exercise regressions and formulas for people." [ROGAN]
- **On the slogans:** "Regression is progression" and "take things from where they are" are not exact phrases I found in these sources. The verified equivalents are "regress any exercise to a comfortable, pain-free level", "meet you where you're at", "gradient scale(s)", and "Everyone can improve... from where you are to where... you are in the future." [ATG][SUB][ROGAN] Commonly cited Ben phrases like "Regression is progression" may appear in his short videos, but I could not verify them here.

### 2.5 Ability vs. demand (his model of pain and injury)
- "Life is a game of the demands upon us vs. our ability to handle them." [SUB, 2026-11-13] He added: "Joints adapt, but if you use too great or too sudden a force, they can get hurt... even I have no guarantee." [SUB]
- The same idea appears in the elitefts profile, where pain comes from demand exceeding ability. [EFTS-BIO]

### 2.6 Range of motion is strength ("strength through flexibility")
- "What I really enjoy is when my strength work and my flexibility work kind of become one and the same... Anywhere I can go, I want to be strong there... that's why I can do the splits cold... because I'm getting strong in those nooks and crannies." He does no foam rolling or massage ("Zero"), and does not currently static stretch, though "I'm not against static stretching." [ROGAN]
- From Poliquin's regret: "the idea of your strength and your flexibility, really getting those into harmony, to where the positions that you're flexible in, you feel strong in those positions." [FERRISS] Ferriss quoted the Poliquinism "Strength is gained in the range it is trained." [FERRISS]
- The "radiating strength" idea (2026): "you DON'T have to force range. If you challenge yourself in the range you're comfortable, then recover from that workout... You just got a little stronger beyond that range!" [SUB, Timeless Ability Above Your Lower Back, 2026-11-21] https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/atg-timeless-ability-above-your-lower
- On sport gaps: "Our legs almost never bend below halfway when we play... The remedy is simple: bring up ability in the range missed by the game." [SUB, Hooper Therapy]

### 2.7 Signature exercises and progressions (verified mentions)
- **Backward sled / backward walking:** "the sled never moves you... When you do squats... The weight is going to move you down." [ROGAN] "if you can walk, you can walk backwards. If you can walk backwards, you could drag a pound backward, two pound, keep going." [ROGAN] Backward up a hill as rehab "rather than going down the hill." [FERRISS]
- **Slant board / step-downs:** "you could roll up a towel to elevate your heel... where you actually work on stepping down... at your pain-free level." [FERRISS] Slant = downhill = more knee pressure, adjusted by off-leg position. [SUB, Slant]
- **Counterbalanced deep squat:** "It's simply a counterbalance... you can actually lean back a bit, your knee doesn't have to go as far over your toes." The progression goes plate held far out, then plate near knees, then kettlebell, then barbell (Zercher/front/back). [FERRISS][SUB] Three squat variables: "How far down? How much weight? WHERE is the weight?" [SUB, Lifetime Squat]
- **ATG split squat** (full-ROM / front-foot-elevated split squat): his mom "credits with fixing her hip problems." [FERRISS]
- **Tibialis raise:** "put your butt against the wall with no equipment whatsoever, raise your toes... a reverse calf raise, a tibialis raise." [FERRISS] He tells a joke story to Rogan about inventing a "chicks dig big tibs" stat for a team ("I lied for their benefit"). [ROGAN]
- **Nordic hamstring curl:** "dating back to the late 1800s... in a sort of gymnastic setting... being able to levitate your body from behind your knee." [ROGAN]
- **Others:** reverse sit-ups/hip-flexor and lower-ab work, sideways back extensions, external rotation and behind-the-neck pressing for shoulders, reverse hyper. [ROGAN][MW]

### 2.8 Patience and frequency over heroics (rehab timeline)
- "I think it would take the average person about five years of consistent work... five years are going to happen whether you like it or not... I'm talking five minutes or less... my average is about a hundred meters a day backward." [ROGAN] Also: "you'd have to put in a hundred miles resisted backwards to know for sure... am I actually doomed or not?" [ROGAN]
- Frequency beats long sessions: "the more frequent... allows you to get that circulation." [ROGAN] "More damage, more you need the sled because it gets the circulation." [ROGAN]
- Biggest mistake: "my biggest mistake was treating it as short-term rehab... and then trying to go back to the methods I used to do." [FERRISS] Rehab and training should be one system: "the idea of physical therapy and exercise physical therapy and strength training staying as one." [MW, pull quote]

### 2.9 Anti-tribalism, "don't see life through a straw"
- "My mentor, Charles Poliquin, stressed: 'Don't see life through a straw.' That's the #1 problem I see in fitness." He notes Louie Simmons, who is wrongly cited as anti-deep-bending, "was training deep bending even more aggressively than I do!" [SUB, 2026-11-13]
- "I don't do the comparing teardown... the program you stick with that works for you and your goals is awesome." [FERRISS]
- "many social media posts try to steer you to one exercise by trashing another... Our next generation needs unbiased history more than they need propaganda." [SUB, Jonathan Edwards, 2026-11-19] https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/atg-jonathan-edwards-timeless-hamstring
- He also stresses integrity: he wants "my posts don't have any lies to try to start arguments," and says "you'll know when I sell out if I'm selling a joint supplement," because supplements "wasn't actually part of my journey." [FERRISS]

### 2.10 Textbooks lag; old rules (RICE, NSAIDs) questioned
- "Everything we're talking about right now in this podcast, without question, will be in the textbooks in 20, 30 years." [ROGAN]
- He says the anti-inflammatories he took "now are looking like actually break down your cartilage," and that the originator of RICE "now says he was wrong... Now he says motion." [ROGAN] (This is his interpretation. The RICE originator, Dr. Gabe Mirkin, did publicly revise his icing advice, but the NSAID/cartilage claim is stronger than the evidence I checked here. Mark it as Ben's view.)

## 4. How he thinks about specific themes (short summary)
- **Pain:** It's a signal to regress, never to push through. Muscle burn is fine, joint pain is not. Pain comes from demand exceeding ability, and the fix is to build ability gradually. [SUB][ROGAN]
- **Injury and damage:** More damage means more need for gentle, frequent, circulation-driving work like backward sled. "there's no such thing as too much damage when it comes to just, okay, if you can walk, you can walk backwards." [ROGAN]
- **Longevity:** Avoiding ranges makes joints fragile. Loaded pressure and full range keep tissue "younger." He wants a childlike squat for life and points to Hackenschmidt as the model. [ROGAN][SUB]
- **Mobility vs. strength:** He rejects the split. Strength through the full range is the mobility work, and flexibility gives access to strength. He stands up for flexibility against young athletes' fear that it costs jumping. [ROGAN][FERRISS]
- **Frequency/volume:** Early on, high frequency with low intensity (daily 100 m backward). Now 1 to 2 working sets and two sessions a week. "the program you stick with... is awesome." [ROGAN][FERRISS]
- **Balance:** Train the neglected links: tibialis, calves, hamstrings (Nordic), hip flexors, lower back, external rotators, "ground up". [FERRISS][ROGAN][SUB]
